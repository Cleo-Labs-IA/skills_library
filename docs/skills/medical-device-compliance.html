EU MDR 2017/745 fully applied from May 2021 and tightened from May 2024 for legacy Directive 93/42 devices. For small medical device companies — class I startups, low-volume orthopedic shops, IVD spin-outs — the MDR is the most expensive regulation the founder will encounter. A Class IIa device commonly costs €80–250k to bring to market under MDR (Notified Body, clinical evaluation, technical file, QMS, post-market surveillance) versus €15–60k under the old MDD.

The medical-device-compliance skill walks through the MDR path: classify the device under Annex VIII Rules 1–22, determine the conformity assessment route under Annex IX/X/XI, select a Notified Body for non-Class I, build the technical documentation per Annex II/III, plan the clinical evaluation per MEDDEV 2.7/1, register on EUDAMED, and structure post-market surveillance under Article 83. It also handles the FDA 510(k) vs De Novo vs PMA decision and the UKCA MDR equivalence.

Why it matters

Class is everything in MDR. A Class I device (e.g. surgical retractor, basic stethoscope) is self-declared. A Class IIa (e.g. dental crown, contact lens solution) requires a Notified Body audit of the QMS plus technical documentation review for a sample. A Class IIb (e.g. infusion pump) requires Notified Body review of the technical documentation for every device. A Class III (e.g. heart valve, implantable defibrillator) requires Notified Body review plus design dossier examination. Misclassify, and the launch slips by 12–18 months.

Notified Body availability is the second bottleneck. Notified Body capacity contracted under MDR (number of designated bodies dropped from 70+ under MDD to ~50). Waiting times for IIa/IIb audit slots can be 8–14 months. The skill helps you understand which Notified Bodies serve your device family and the realistic lead time.

EUDAMED partial activation has staggered key transparency obligations through 2025–2027. The Actor and UDI/Device modules went mandatory in February 2026; Clinical Investigation, Vigilance and Market Surveillance modules are following. The skill flags which modules apply to your device today and which obligations are still in voluntary/transition phase.

How the medical-device-compliance skill works

  1. Classify the device. Annex VIII Rules 1–22. Skill walks through the rule that drives the highest class (the highest applicable rule wins). Common surprises: software classification has its own Rule 11, contact-with-mucosa rules upgrade many products one class.
  2. Choose conformity assessment route. Annex IX (full QMS + technical documentation) for IIa/IIb/III; Annex X (type examination) for some III; Annex XI for production quality assurance. Class I has self-declaration route except sterile, measuring, or reusable surgical.
  3. Pick a Notified Body. Required for Class IIa and above. Cross-check the NB's scope (MDR codes) against your device. Confirm lead time. Typical fees: €25–80k for IIa first audit, €60–180k for IIb/III.
  4. Build the technical file. Annex II + III. Device description, design info, risk management (ISO 14971), V&V testing, biocompatibility (ISO 10993), labels/IFU, clinical evaluation, post-market plan.
  5. Clinical evaluation. MEDDEV 2.7/1 rev 4 + MDCG 2020-13. Literature route, equivalence route, or clinical investigation. Equivalence narrower under MDR than MDD.
  6. Register on EUDAMED. Actor module mandatory Feb 2026. UDI/Device module also mandatory. Vigilance and Market Surveillance modules in transition.
  7. Run post-market surveillance. Article 83. PMS plan, PMCF if Class IIa+, PSUR (Class IIa annually for higher class, otherwise as needed). Vigilance reporting under Article 87 (10 working days for serious incident).

FAQ

What is EU MDR compliance?

EU MDR compliance is the set of obligations under Regulation 2017/745 that every medical device placed on the EU market must satisfy: classification under Annex VIII, conformity assessment under the appropriate Annex, technical documentation under Annex II/III, clinical evaluation, QMS under ISO 13485, UDI/EUDAMED registration, and post-market surveillance under Article 83.

How long does Notified Body certification take?

Under MDR, typical lead time from submission to certificate is 9–14 months for a Class IIa device, 12–18 months for Class IIb/III. The bottleneck is Notified Body capacity, which contracted under MDR (~50 designated bodies down from 70+ under MDD).

Is EUDAMED mandatory?

Yes for the Actor and UDI/Device modules, mandatory since February 2026. Clinical Investigation, Vigilance and Market Surveillance modules are in staged activation through 2027. Full mandatory use of all modules is now in force.

Can I use MDD legacy certificates?

Reg 2023/607 extended the MDD legacy certificates through December 2027 for Class III and IIb implants, and through December 2028 for Class IIa, IIb non-implant and Class I sterile/measuring/reusable surgical — subject to conditions in the regulation.

Does the skill cover IVDR?

Yes, alongside MDR. The IVDR (Reg 2017/746) classification (Class A–D), conformity routes, and EUDAMED IVD obligations are covered in the same skill.

How does FDA 510(k) compare to MDR?

FDA 510(k) is the US predicate-based path for moderate-risk devices (similar to MDR Class IIa/IIb scope but with a substantial equivalence standard rather than performance-against-essential-requirements). Typical FDA 510(k) review 4–7 months vs MDR Notified Body 9–14 months.

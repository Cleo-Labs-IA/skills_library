EU cosmetics compliance is the most well-documented and least understood regulatory regime an indie brand will ever encounter. The text of Regulation 1223/2009 runs 36 articles and 8 annexes, but the practical path from a finished formula to a live CPNP notification involves at least six gates: classification, formula screen against Annexes II–VI, Responsible Person appointment, Product Information File, Cosmetic Product Safety Report, and label per Article 19. Miss any one and the product is unsellable — and Amazon, Sephora and Douglas now check.

The cosmetics-compliance skill walks you through all six gates in sequence, with the specific articles cited, the typical fees for each step, and the timeline you should budget. It also extends to UK SCPN (post-Brexit), US FDA MoCRA (2024+), Japan MHLW quasi-drug rules, Korea MFDS notification, China NMPA, and the eight-country ASEAN cosmetic harmonization.

Why it matters

Almost every indie brand we have spoken to launches a face product, gets European traction through Amazon EU or a marketplace like Sephora's third-party offer, and only then discovers that the product needs a CPSR signed by a qualified safety assessor. At that point the launch is paused for 4–6 weeks while the brand finds an assessor, builds the PIF, and pays €1,000–4,000 per SKU. Almost all of that delay is preventable by treating the CPSR as a launch dependency, not an afterthought.

The skill flips the timing. You run the formula through the EU annex screen before you order packaging. You appoint a Responsible Person before your first EU customs entry. You assemble the PIF in parallel with bottle artwork. By the time the product is ready to ship, the notification is live and the CPSR signed.

It also handles the moving parts. The 2024 retinol cap. The lilial ban from August 2022 that some assessors still miss. The new 80-allergen list under Reg 2023/1545 with the 2026–2028 transition. The skill flags every Annex update that affects your specific formula, with the citation and the substitution path.

How the cosmetics-compliance skill works

  1. Confirm classification. Borderline products (SPF, hair growth, anti-acne, probiotic, intimate care, baby) get classified as cosmetic, biocide, medical device, or quasi-drug depending on the claim and the ingredient. The skill checks all four borders.
  2. Screen the formula. Every ingredient at every concentration is checked against Annexes II (banned), III (restricted), IV (colorants), V (preservatives), VI (UV filters). Common blockers: BHT >0.8%, lilial post-2022, retinol >0.3% face, methylisothiazolinone in leave-on, BHA >0.001%.
  3. Appoint a Responsible Person. The skill compares EU RP providers, costs (€100–600/month), CPNP-management rights, accessory market notification, and contract terms. Same flow for UK RP.
  4. Build the PIF. Product Information File requires the formula, stability tests, microbiology challenge test, packaging migration data, claims substantiation, RIPT, plus the CPSR Part B. The skill outputs a complete PIF skeleton.
  5. Sign the CPSR. Part A is structured documentation (physical/chemical, microbiology, exposure, toxicology profile, undesirable effects). Part B is the assessor's signed safety conclusion. The skill prepares Part A; a qualified human signs Part B.
  6. Notify on CPNP. Upload via your RP before placing on market. Notification is free, takes 15 minutes, and is mandatory even for re-import or relaunch.
  7. Label per Article 19. Mandatory: RP address, batch, function, warnings, INCI descending order, allergens above thresholds, country of origin if outside EU, PAO or expiry, nominal content.

FAQ

What is EU cosmetics compliance?

EU cosmetics compliance is the body of obligations under Regulation 1223/2009 that any product placed on the EU market must satisfy: classification as a cosmetic, formula against Annexes II–VI, appointment of an EU-established Responsible Person, Product Information File, Cosmetic Product Safety Report signed by a qualified safety assessor, notification on the CPNP portal, and Article 19 labeling.

Do I need an EU Responsible Person if I sell only on Amazon UK?

For UK sales you need a UK Responsible Person under UK Cosmetics Regulation (SCPN portal). For EU sales (DE, FR, IT, etc.) you need a separate EU RP. The two regimes are independent post-Brexit. Many indie brands appoint one provider that covers both for ~€150–400/month combined.

How much does a CPSR cost?

A CPSR signed by a qualified safety assessor typically costs €400–1500 per product for simple formulations and €1500–4000 for complex products with active ingredients, leave-on facial, or children's products. It must be updated whenever the formula or claims change materially.

What changed with the 2024 Annex III amendments?

Retinol face products are capped at 0.3% (body 0.05%) per Reg 2024/996, applicable November 2025. Three salicylate esters were added to Annex III in late 2024. Methylsalicylate was restricted for use in children's products. The skill flags these automatically.

Can the skill replace a safety assessor?

No. Article 10 requires the CPSR Part B to be signed by a person with a recognized pharmacy, toxicology, medicine or related diploma. The skill builds the structured Part A documentation that the assessor uses as input, cutting assessor time by 50–70 percent.

Does it cover China NMPA?

Yes — China NMPA registration (general use cosmetics post-2021 reform), new ingredient filing, RP appointment in China, and the animal testing exemption are all covered. The skill flags ingredients that still require animal testing in China for special-use cosmetics (hair dye, sunscreen, anti-hair-loss).

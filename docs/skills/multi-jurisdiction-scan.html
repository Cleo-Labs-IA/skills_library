Multi-country product launches break in predictable ways. A face cream sells beautifully in France, gets traction in Germany, the founder decides to go to the US — and discovers MoCRA needs a six-week facility registration window. The launch slips. The marketing budget shifts. The momentum disappears. The same problem repeats in reverse: a US brand opens EU sales without a Responsible Person and Amazon EU removes the listing within 4 weeks of go-live.

The multi-jurisdiction-scan skill runs each market check in parallel via superpowers:dispatching-parallel-agents. You give it the product spec and the candidate market list, and one agent per jurisdiction (or jurisdiction cluster) runs in parallel. The total wall-clock is dominated by the slowest market check, not the sum — so 8 markets run in about 12 minutes versus 90 minutes sequential.

Why it matters

The expensive multi-market mistake is not missing a regulation. It is sequencing the launches wrong. A founder who knows in week 1 that the EU+UK+US launch needs an EU RP, a UK RP, a US FDA facility registration, and 3 separate CPNP/SCPN/MoCRA notifications can sequence the work in parallel and ship in 8 weeks. A founder who learns these requirements one at a time as customs and Amazon flag them takes 6–9 months and burns the launch.

Parallel scanning surfaces the critical-path market. For most consumer brands, the critical path is either the EU CPSR (cosmetics, 4–6 weeks) or the FDA Notified Body equivalent (medical, 9–14 months Notified Body for IIa+). The skill identifies the critical path explicitly so the operator can resource it first.

Markets can be deprioritised cleanly. A Korea launch that requires functional cosmetic review for a single SKU may not be worth the effort if Korea is 8 percent of forecast revenue. The skill returns a Revenue-at-Risk and Time-to-Compliance estimate per market so the founder can make that call with numbers.

How the multi-jurisdiction-scan skill works

  1. Define product and candidate markets. Product spec + ingredient list / BOM + candidate market list. Common combos: EU+UK+US, EU+US+JP+KR, full G7, EU+US+JP+CN+ASEAN.
  2. Dispatch one agent per jurisdiction cluster. Skill spawns parallel agents via superpowers:dispatching-parallel-agents. One agent per market or cluster (e.g. ASEAN as one, Nordic as one).
  3. Each agent runs full product-compliance + market-entry-checklist. Substance screen, label compliance, RP requirements, testing/certification, customs path, marketplace gates.
  4. Aggregate to RED/ORANGE/YELLOW/GREEN per market. RED = legally blocked, requires reformulation. ORANGE = blocked without specific action (CPSR, registration). YELLOW = additional documentation needed. GREEN = ship as is.
  5. Calculate Revenue-at-Risk per market. Forecast revenue share per market × probability of issue × time-to-fix = revenue at risk.
  6. Surface the critical path. Identify the market that drives the latest launch date and the action that resolves it. Output: ranked sequencing plan.

FAQ

What is a multi-jurisdiction scan?

It is a parallel compliance check across all your target markets simultaneously, returning a RED / ORANGE / YELLOW / GREEN verdict per market with the specific blockers cited. The skill dispatches one agent per market via Claude's parallel-agents primitive.

How long does it take?

Wall-clock is dominated by the slowest market check, not the sum. Typical 6–10 markets run in 10–15 minutes. The equivalent sequential check would take 90–180 minutes.

How many markets does it cover?

Full coverage for EU 27, UK, US (federal + key states), Japan, Korea, China, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, ASEAN 10, GCC countries, Brazil. Coverage extending to Mexico, India, South Africa in 2025.

Does it pick the right launch sequence?

Yes. The output includes a critical-path analysis: which market drives the longest launch lead time and which markets can be deferred without losing forecast revenue. The skill optimises for both time-to-market and revenue capture.

How does it interact with the product-compliance skill?

The product-compliance skill is the per-product engine; the multi-jurisdiction-scan is the per-market dispatcher. The dispatcher invokes product-compliance + market-entry-checklist + customs-and-trade + responsible-person inside each market agent.

Can it identify which markets to deprioritise?

Yes. The Revenue-at-Risk × Time-to-Compliance matrix highlights markets where forecast revenue does not justify the compliance investment, so the founder can rationally drop a market and reallocate budget.

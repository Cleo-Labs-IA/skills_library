FDA food labeling compliance is one of the most documented compliance regimes anywhere — 21 CFR 101 runs to several hundred pages, the Food Labeling Guide is a 132-page PDF, and the FSMA preventive controls rules add another 80,000 words on top. And yet small brands routinely launch with a non-compliant Nutrition Facts panel, miss the sesame allergen rule that took effect in January 2023, or skip the FDA biennial facility registration that triggers FSMA enforcement.

The food-compliance skill is built for the brand that has a great recipe, a co-packer, and a launch date — and now needs to produce a label and a food safety plan that survives FDA inspection and an Amazon Brand Registry challenge. It covers the US label panel, FALCPA + FASTER Act allergens, FDA facility registration, the Preventive Controls (HACCP-aligned) food safety plan, EU FIC labeling, Japan FFC functional foods, Korea HFF, and Canada CFIA.

Why it matters

A non-compliant Nutrition Facts panel is the single most common reason an indie food brand gets a Warning Letter from FDA — not adulteration, not allergen contamination, but a wrong serving size, a missing added-sugar line, or a Class II nutrient declared inaccurately. The 2016 panel update added mandatory disclosure for added sugars, vitamin D and potassium, and updated DV reference values. Brands still using pre-2016 panels are out of compliance.

The 9th allergen is the second most common gap. The FASTER Act added sesame as a mandatory allergen on January 1, 2023. Any product sold in the US that contains sesame and does not declare it in plain English under FALCPA is non-compliant and a recall candidate. The skill flags sesame in 30+ trade names (tahini, sesamol, gingelly oil, til, benne) where it hides.

FSMA preventive controls is the third. Almost every facility manufacturing, processing, packing or holding food for US consumption must have a written food safety plan with hazard analysis, preventive controls, monitoring, verification, recall plan, and records. Co-packers usually have this — but brand-owners are responsible for ensuring the co-packer is compliant.

How the food-compliance skill works

  1. Classify the product. Conventional food, dietary supplement, medical food, or infant formula? Each has a different label format and a different regulatory section. The skill checks structure-function claims that may push a food into the supplement category.
  2. Build the Nutrition Facts panel. Use 21 CFR 101.9. Mandatory nutrients post-2016: total sugars, added sugars, vitamin D, calcium, iron, potassium. Calculate serving size by RACC reference amount.
  3. Declare allergens (FALCPA + FASTER). 9 majors: milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soy, sesame. Plain English in ingredient list or via a contains statement.
  4. Check Prop 65 (California). ~900 listed substances. Most relevant for food: lead, acrylamide, BPA, furfuryl alcohol. Online warning + on-pack warning if threshold exceeded.
  5. Register the facility (FSMA). FDA facility registration is biennial. Required for any facility manufacturing, processing, packing or holding food for US consumption — including foreign facilities.
  6. Apply preventive controls. 21 CFR 117 Subpart C: hazard analysis, preventive controls, monitoring, verification, supply-chain controls, recall plan, records.
  7. Country-of-origin marking. 19 CFR 134 + USDA COOL for covered commodities. EU mandates origin for beef, pork, poultry, sheep, goat, honey, olive oil, plus voluntary for other categories.

FAQ

What is FDA food labeling compliance?

FDA food labeling compliance is the set of labeling rules under 21 CFR 101 (and FDA FSMA for facility-level food safety) that any food sold in the US must follow: Statement of Identity, net quantity, Nutrition Facts panel, ingredient list, allergen statement, manufacturer information, and any required Prop 65 warning.

Is sesame now a mandatory allergen?

Yes. Sesame became the 9th major allergen under the FASTER Act starting January 1, 2023. It must be declared in plain English under FALCPA either in the ingredient list or via a contains statement.

Do I need a Nutrition Facts panel for a single-ingredient product?

Most foods sold at retail require a Nutrition Facts panel. Exemptions: very small businesses (under USD 500k/yr food sales OR under USD 50k food sales), foods served for immediate consumption, raw produce and fish. Single-ingredient sugars must use the added-sugar declaration.

What is the difference between FDA FSMA and HACCP?

HACCP is the international risk-based food safety methodology (Codex). FSMA is the US implementation framework — 21 CFR 117 Subpart C requires a Preventive Controls plan that is HACCP-aligned but adds supply-chain controls, recall plans, and FDA inspection rights.

Does the skill cover EU food labeling too?

Yes. EU FIC Regulation 1169/2011 (mandatory information, allergen highlighting, country-of-origin), EU 1924/2006 health claims, and EU 2015/2283 novel foods are all covered alongside FDA. The same skill handles both regimes side by side.

What about UK Natasha's Law (PPDS)?

UK Natasha's Law (PPDS — pre-packed for direct sale) requires the 14 priority allergens to appear on any food packed on the same premises from which it is sold. The skill flags products falling under PPDS and applies the corresponding label format.

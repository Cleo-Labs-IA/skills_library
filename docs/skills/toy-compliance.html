EN 71 testing is the European toy safety standard suite that gives presumption of conformity to the EU Toy Safety Directive 2009/48/EC (being replaced by the new Toy Safety Regulation, expected to apply from 2027). For US, the equivalent is CPSIA + ASTM F963; for China, CCC + GB 6675; for Japan, the ST Mark scheme.

The toy-compliance skill maps each part of EN 71 — mechanical (EN 71-1), flammability (EN 71-2), chemical migration (EN 71-3), experimental sets (EN 71-4), other chemical (EN 71-5), age grading (EN 71-6 + 71-7), playground (EN 71-8), organic compounds (EN 71-9 to 11), electrical (EN 62115) — to your specific toy and lists the labs and typical costs per test.

Why it matters

Toys are the single most-recalled category in the EU Safety Gate and CPSC. The most common recall causes are small parts hazards for under-3 toys, button-cell battery accessibility, magnet ingestion risk, and chemical migration (phthalates, lead, cadmium). Each of these is detectable in standard EN 71 / ASTM F963 testing for €1,500–6,000 per toy.

The new EU Toy Safety Regulation, expected to enter into force in 2026 with a 30-month transition, replaces the Directive. Key changes: digital product passport mandatory; explicit bans on PFAS in toys; chemical migration tightened to align with REACH SVHC concentration limits; importer obligations expanded; expanded scope to toys with software/connectivity.

Amazon and other marketplaces have started requesting test reports proactively for toys, especially under-3 categories. Listings without a recent EN 71 / ASTM F963 test report are at risk of removal even without a specific complaint.

How the toy-compliance skill works

  1. Classify the toy and age grade. Toy or non-toy? EU Toy Safety Directive applies to products designed or intended for use in play by children under 14. Age grading per EN 71-6: <3, 3+, 8+, 14+.
  2. Map the EN 71 parts that apply. EN 71-1 mechanical & physical (universal). EN 71-2 flammability (textiles, costumes, masks). EN 71-3 chemical migration of 19 elements (any toy with paint/coating). EN 62115 electrical (if powered).
  3. Run the testing. Send to ISO 17025 accredited lab. Typical timeline: 3–4 weeks. Typical cost EN 71-1+2+3 + EN 62115: €2,500–5,000 per SKU.
  4. Compile the technical file. Description, drawings, BOM, test reports, risk assessment per Annex II, DoC, age warning labels, traceability batch system.
  5. Affix CE mark and write the EU DoC. CE on product or packaging, in EU language(s) of destination, with all directives and standards listed including EN 71 parts.
  6. Handle US: CPSIA + ASTM F963. CPSIA: lead 100 ppm content (not just migration), 8 phthalates 0.1%, tracking labels. ASTM F963: mechanical, flammability, heavy metal soluble migration.
  7. Run the China and Japan paths. China CCC required for 6 toy categories under GB 6675 (rolling toys, ride-ons, dolls, electric toys, plastic toys, metal toys). JP ST Mark is voluntary but retailer-required.

FAQ

What is EN 71 testing?

EN 71 is the European toy safety standard suite, divided into 11+ parts that give presumption of conformity to the EU Toy Safety Directive 2009/48/EC. The most-tested parts are EN 71-1 (mechanical), EN 71-2 (flammability), and EN 71-3 (migration of 19 elements from coating). Cost per SKU typically €1,500–5,000.

Is CE the same as EN 71?

No. CE is the manufacturer self-declaration that the toy meets the Toy Safety Directive. EN 71 is the harmonised standard you use to demonstrate that conformity. You can in principle achieve CE without EN 71 (by demonstrating compliance another way), but in practice every toy uses EN 71 because it gives the presumption of conformity.

What changed with the new EU Toy Safety Regulation?

The new TSR, expected to apply from 2027, replaces Directive 2009/48/EC. Key changes: digital product passport mandatory; explicit PFAS bans; chemical migration aligned with REACH SVHC; importer obligations expanded; expanded scope to toys with software/connectivity; broader endocrine-disruptor restriction in toys.

What is Reese's Law?

US Public Law 117-171 (2022) requires child-resistant compartments and warning labels on any consumer product with a button cell or coin cell battery. Triggered by ingestion fatalities. ANSI/UL 4200A specifies the compartment performance test.

Do I need a Children's Product Certificate?

Yes for the US. CPSIA Section 102 requires manufacturers and importers of children's products (designed for children 12 and under) to certify compliance with applicable rules based on third-party CPSC-accepted lab testing. The CPC is issued by the manufacturer/importer.

Does the skill cover toy-adjacent products (children's textiles, baby products)?

For children's textiles use the textile-compliance skill (CPSIA + REACH for textiles + flammability). For baby products (cots, strollers, high chairs) use baby-children-products-compliance — different EN standards.

CE marking electronics looks like a single mark on the back of the device. It is actually a stacked compliance regime: LVD for electrical safety, EMC for electromagnetic compatibility, RED for any product with a radio (Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, NFC, GPS), RoHS for restricted substances, ErP for energy-related products, EU Battery Regulation for any product with a built-in or removable battery, and from 2027 the Cyber Resilience Act for products with digital elements.

The electronics-compliance skill walks through each directive that applies to your specific product, identifies the right harmonized standards (EN 60335 family for appliances, EN 55032 for EMC, EN 300 328 for 2.4 GHz radios), and produces the EU Declaration of Conformity structure. It also handles the US equivalent path: FCC Part 15 Subpart B unintentional + Subpart C intentional, UL listing where retailer-required, and California Energy Commission appliance standards.

Why it matters

A consumer electronics product launched without proper CE testing has roughly a 30 percent chance of being pulled from Amazon EU within 12 months of launch — usually via a competitor or watchdog complaint that triggers a marketplace audit. The retailer requires the test reports and DoC, not just the mark itself.

The Cyber Resilience Act adds a new dimension. From late 2027, any product with a digital element placed on the EU market must satisfy security-by-design requirements, vulnerability handling obligations, and reporting duties. This applies whether the product has Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or just a USB firmware update path.

EU Battery Regulation 2023/1542 replaces the old Battery Directive and applies in waves through 2027. Passport requirements for industrial and EV batteries from 2026; due diligence on cobalt, lithium, nickel, and natural graphite from 2025; carbon footprint declaration mandatory from 2026 onward.

How the electronics-compliance skill works

  1. Identify all applicable directives. Most consumer electronics products trigger at least 4 CE directives at once. The skill maps function and components to the directive list — LVD, EMC, RED, RoHS, ErP, Battery, CRA where applicable.
  2. Pick the right harmonized standards. For each directive, pick the harmonized EN standard that gives presumption of conformity. The skill matches product class to standard (EN 60335 vs EN 62368, EN 55032 vs CISPR 25, EN 300 328 for 2.4 GHz).
  3. Run the conformity assessment. Internal Production Control (Module A) for most consumer electronics; Notified Body for Class III medical or where harmonized standards are absent.
  4. Compile the technical file. Description, drawings, BOM, test reports, risk assessment, EU DoC. Retain 10 years post last unit placed on market.
  5. Affix CE mark and write the DoC. CE on product or rating plate, DoC drafted in EU official language of the market, with all directives and standards listed.
  6. Mirror for UKCA. UKCA mark required for GB market. UKCA standards designated by gov.uk; effectively the same as EN for now but diverging. Northern Ireland still on CE.
  7. Handle FCC for US market. Part 15 Subpart B for unintentional radiators (any digital device); Subpart C for intentional radiators (Wi-Fi, BT). SDoC (self-declaration) or full certification depending on type.

FAQ

What is CE marking for electronics?

CE is the EU manufacturer self-declaration that the product meets all applicable EU product directives (LVD, EMC, RED, RoHS, ErP, Battery, CRA as applicable) and supporting harmonized standards. It is required for placing electronics on the EU market.

Do I need a Notified Body for CE on electronics?

Usually no. Most consumer electronics use Module A (self-declaration) and need no Notified Body. Notified Body involvement is required for radio equipment using non-harmonized standards (RED Module B+C), high-power radio, or specific medical/PPE-adjacent products.

What changed with RED Article 3.3 in 2025?

From August 2025, internet-connected radio devices, wearables, and toys with radio must satisfy three new essential requirements: network resilience, personal data protection, and protection against fraud. Harmonized standards EN 18031-1/-2/-3 give presumption of conformity.

Is UKCA still required?

Yes for GB market — but the UK Product Regulation and Metrology Bill 2024 extends indefinite recognition of CE for most product categories. UKCA is still valid; CE is sufficient for GB in most categories as of 2025.

What is the EU Cyber Resilience Act?

Reg 2024/2847, applicable Dec 2027 for most products. Any product with a digital element placed on the EU market must meet security-by-design, vulnerability handling, and 24h-incident reporting requirements. Penalties up to 2.5% of global turnover.

INCI labeling looks deceptively simple — list ingredients in descending order of concentration, declare allergens, add warnings, you are done. In practice, the order math is wrong for at least one ingredient in roughly half the labels we audit, the allergen list is missing entries that should appear, and the multi-language rules for Belgium, Switzerland, Canada and ASEAN are routinely ignored.

The labeling-compliance skill takes your formula and target markets and outputs a compliant label per market: INCI descending order with the right threshold for the and other ingredients break point at 1%, the allergens above 0.001% leave-on / 0.01% rinse-off in the right language(s), the RP/PIF holder address, the batch code position, the PAO symbol, country of origin, and the required warnings (sunscreens, hair dyes, AHA, etc.).

Why it matters

A non-compliant INCI label is the single most common reason a cosmetic product gets a market surveillance challenge in the EU. Most challenges are not about the formula — they are about wrong INCI order, missing allergens, untranslated warnings, or an unreadable batch code.

Reg 2023/1545 expanded the EU fragrance allergen list from 26 to roughly 80 substances. The transition is staged: new products from July 2026, existing products from July 2028. Every brand that touches fragrance needs to re-audit their labels.

Multi-language rules are easy to miss. Belgium requires French, Dutch, and German on every cosmetic label. Switzerland requires French, German, and Italian. Canada requires English and French. ASEAN harmonization allows the local language plus English for most member states.

How the labeling-compliance skill works

  1. Resolve trade names to INCI. Every ingredient on the spec sheet must appear as its INCI name. The skill resolves trade names (Sepiplus 400, Olivem 1000) to their INCI components in descending concentration.
  2. Apply the descending-order rule. Ingredients above 1% in descending concentration. Below 1% in any order. Colorants at the end prefixed by + or in a sub-list.
  3. Identify allergens above threshold. Old 26-list: must appear in INCI if >0.001% leave-on or >0.01% rinse-off. New 80-list (Reg 2023/1545) applies from July 2026 / 2028.
  4. Add mandatory information. RP/PIF address, batch number position, function, country of origin if outside EU, nominal content, PAO or expiry, warnings.
  5. Translate to all required languages. EU Member State language(s) for the warnings + INCI common (cosmetic-specific exemption: INCI list is in INCI English/Latin only).
  6. Position the label correctly. Some info on the container (RP, batch, PAO), some on the box (full INCI if container too small via the leaflet/joined symbol).
  7. Validate against destination. EU 1223/2009 Art. 19, UK Cosmetics Reg Schedule 1 (mirrors EU + post-Brexit RP), US MoCRA 2024 (fragrance allergens by 2025), JP MHLW (full Japanese), KR MFDS, CN NMPA (Chinese), CA bilingual.

FAQ

What is INCI labeling?

INCI = International Nomenclature of Cosmetic Ingredients. It is the standardised name list (English / Latin) used on cosmetic labels worldwide, defined in CosIng (EU) and by the Personal Care Products Council (US). EU Reg 1223/2009 Article 19 mandates INCI on the EU market.

What is the new 80-allergen list?

Reg 2023/1545 expanded the EU mandatory fragrance allergen labeling list from 26 to approximately 80 substances. Transition: new products placed on market from 31 July 2026; existing stock from 31 July 2028. The same expansion is being mirrored by UK SCPN and by FDA under MoCRA fragrance disclosure.

Do small containers need full INCI?

If the container is too small for full INCI, the full list can appear on a leaflet attached to the product (the open-book symbol must appear on the container). Common for 5-15 ml lipsticks, mascaras and sample sizes.

What is the order rule below 1%?

Above 1% concentration: descending order. Below 1%: any order. Colorants must be listed at the end of the ingredient list (prefixed by + or in a separate group).

Does my US label need INCI?

Yes for cosmetics — FDA 21 CFR 701.3 mandates ingredient declaration in descending order of predominance, using the INCI common name. MoCRA from 2025 will additionally require fragrance allergen disclosure on US labels.

Does it cover supplements and food labels too?

Use the dedicated food-compliance and supplement-compliance skills for those — they apply different rules (Nutrition Facts panel, FALCPA allergens, DSHEA disclaimers). This skill is cosmetic-focused.
